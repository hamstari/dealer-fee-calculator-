4 One price for everyone

Many lender agreements (and some state laws) don't let you charge financed customers more than cash customers. Instead, build the expected fees into a single price for all customers.

How the math works

  • Financed price = cash price ÷ (1 − dealer fee). With a 10% fee on a $10,000 job, charge $11,111 — the lender keeps $1,111 and you net $10,000.
  • Adding the fee on top (×1.10 = $11,000) doesn't work: the lender takes 10% of the bigger number, and you net only $9,900.
  • With a cash down payment, the fee only applies to the financed part.
  • Margin here is a percentage of the selling price. A 20% margin equals a 25% markup on cost.
  • Monthly payments are simple amortized estimates. Deferred-interest and "same as cash" plans bill differently, so use the lender's figures when quoting payments.

For estimating only. Check your lender agreement and local rules before you change how you price financed jobs.
